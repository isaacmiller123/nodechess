# d10

FEEL: Right-edge binder tabs
Intuitiveness: learnable. All five destinations are on screen at all times, labelled,
with no hover reveal and no menu, but they stand on the right edge with their labels
set vertically, so it costs one glance to learn and then it is muscle memory.
Navigation: five binder dividers on the right edge; the current one is pulled out and
its paper is continuous with the page, and the page keeps a printed left margin where
notes hang beside the thing they point at.
Motion: the ink draws itself once, left to right, as if written; tabs slide out a few
pixels under the cursor; nothing else moves.
Mobile: the dividers move to the bottom edge and stand up as flap tabs under the thumb,
the margin rule narrows to 14px but stays, and every control grows rather than shrinks.

STYLE: Graphite field notebook
Palette:
  #1c1e19 cover board, the surface the sheet lies on
  #2a2d26 sheet, every page and panel
  #e4e1d4 pencil, primary text on toned paper
  #e0b52e field yellow, the only thing worth pressing
  #e0665c red pencil, annotation ink and errors
  #7ea6d4 blue pencil, the second pass and the printed ruling
Type: display Roboto Slab, body IBM Plex Sans, mono IBM Plex Mono. Notation, labels and
marginalia are all mono because that is how a scorebook is printed, and the slab gives
headings the weight of a stamped form. Those three files would ship self-hosted (Apache
2.0 and OFL 1.1); until then each stack falls back to a face with the same skeleton
(Superclarendon, Segoe UI, Menlo) so nothing lands silently on system-ui.
Icons: d10 Pen Set, 12 icons drawn for this direction, 24px box, 1.5 stroke, open ends
and no closed counters, so they sit next to hand ink without looking manufactured.
Inlined as one sprite, nothing to license.
Signature: the ink layer. The primary action is circled, a margin note fires an arrow
into the page, and on the lesson board Viktor's arrow runs from c4 to f7 with a ring
round the square. Chess players already annotate in exactly this language.

Combines well with: d01 and d05. Both are quiet and typographic, so the ink stays the
only raised voice. d05's zero radius turns the sheet into a printed form, which suits
the margin. Verified by swapping both style files under these pages.
Fights with: d08, whose cyan accent leaves my red ink reading as an error rather than a
hand; and any style with a soft, high-radius, glowing surface, since a notebook page
that floats is a contradiction.
