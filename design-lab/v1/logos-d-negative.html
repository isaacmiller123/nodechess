idea A triangular peer lattice whose nodes swell and link only where the network holds consensus, and the region where it holds is a rook. Outside it the peers thin to dust. The three merlons are three standalone peers tethered to the tower, so the crenellations you read as chess are the same dots you read as machines.

idea One closed circuit visiting twenty three peers. The circuit is drawn, the queen is not: she is the void the route encloses, and the five coronet points are literally the five largest nodes on it. The hairlines crossing the interior are witnesses, chords of the same cycle.

reduction Filled the void the circuit was enclosing and dropped the chords and the minor vertices. The five coronet nodes survive as five separate dots, so the crown still reads as a counted set and not as a serrated edge.

idea No node holds the whole game. A king is cut into nine horizontal shards, each held at a slightly different offset by a different peer, and your eye does the reassembly the network does. The seams are the mesh, and they are the only thing drawn that is not the piece.

reduction Went from nine shards to three and widened the seams to a full pixel so they survive. The offsets alternate rather than drift, which keeps the fracture legible as intent at sixteen pixels.

idea Twenty four sectors, one per peer, closed around a bishop that is never drawn. The rim is uneven because peers are not equal, the seams run all the way to the centre, and the bishop exists only as the shape they all leave alone. The two bands crossing the void are the mesh passing behind the piece.

reduction Removed the seams, the uneven rim and the mitre slit, keeping the disc and the void. The gap between mitre and body is held at one pixel so the piece still reads as two parts, which is what stops it becoming a keyhole.
