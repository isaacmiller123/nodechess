lane a · the knight’s tour

nodechess

A knight’s tour is a Hamiltonian path over a graph whose vertices are squares. It is chess and it is network topology at the same time, with no metaphor in between, which is why every mark here is built from real knight moves on real board coordinates rather than from a picture of a network. Two tiers throughout: a primary mark with genuine detail for 24px and up, and a reduction of that same mark for 16px.

idea. The closed knight’s tour of the 3×3 board: eight peers, eight knight moves, one unbroken ring, and a hole in the middle because the centre square is the one square no knight can ever reach.

reduced for 16px. Removed the interlace and the eight node dots. The same eight chords remain, drawn flat, heavier, and pushed further out so the centre hole survives at 16px.

idea. A partial tour with the stroke growing move by move, so you can read direction and elapsed time in the line itself; the fifth move crosses the first, which is how you know the board keeps going past the frame.

reduced for 16px. Removed the taper and the six square markers. The same five moves at one uniform weight, opened out slightly so the angles stay open at 16px.

idea. Two knight moves drawn as the routes they actually take spell the letter n; the bead sits on the square where the two moves meet, the square that witnesses both.

reduced for 16px. Removed the hairline chords in the counter and the two foot nodes. The n and its crown bead remain, at a heavier weight and a wider stance.

idea. A single knight move at full size: the bend is the chess move and the bend is the network edge, the same object seen twice, with hairlines showing the tour arriving and leaving because the network does not end at the frame.

reduced for 16px. Removed the two hairline continuations. The bend and its two landing squares remain, thickened and squared up to fill the box.
